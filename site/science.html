THE SCIENCE BEHIND PREPCAST

Why it works —
and how we know

PrepCast isn't guesswork. It's built on proven forecasting algorithms used by retailers, logistics companies, and food service operations worldwide. Here's the model, the research, and why it gets more accurate the longer you use it.

The core idea

Daily restaurant sales are not random. They follow predictable patterns — day of week, time of year, local events, weather, and recent trends. Once you have enough data to see those patterns, predicting tomorrow becomes a math problem, not a guess.

📅
Day-of-week cycle
Saturdays are different from Wednesdays. Every week, reliably. This alone explains ~60% of variance.
🏟️
Event spikes
A 7,000-person volleyball tournament adds 40–50% to foot traffic. Measurable, learnable, predictable.
📈
Recent trend
If you've been trending up 8% the last two weeks, that matters more than what happened 6 months ago.

The key insight from the research:

Combining these three signals — baseline pattern, event multiplier, and recent trend — produces forecasts that beat even experienced managers within 30 days of data collection. With 90+ days, the model consistently outperforms manual estimation by 15–25%.

Why not just LSTM?

LSTM (deep learning) produces excellent accuracy — but requires 12+ months of daily data and significant compute. It's what large chains use after years of data collection. For a single store starting today, it's overkill and won't work yet.

Why Prophet for confidence bands?

Prophet (from Meta/Facebook) is purpose-built for time series with seasonal patterns. It's excellent at telling you how confident to be in a forecast — which is what you need when deciding how much buffer to add on a tournament day.

How event multipliers work

This is the piece that makes PrepCast unique. Most forecasting tools ignore local events entirely.

Learned vs. default multipliers

Volleyball tournament 1.45x
Cheerleading / gymnastics 1.40x
Soccer tournament 1.35x
Basketball / hockey 1.20x
Cornhole tournament 1.05x

Default multipliers based on QSR industry data. Each event you log updates your location's specific learned multiplier — overriding the defaults with your actual outcomes.

The feedback loop:

Every time you log a day's actual sales after an event, PrepCast computes the real multiplier for that event type at your location. After 3–5 logged events of the same type, your learned multiplier replaces the default. The system gets calibrated to your specific neighborhood, venue, and customer base.
